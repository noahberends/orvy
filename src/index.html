A step sequencer driven by the Game of Life

Draw cells on the grid and press Play. Each lit cell plays its row's note when the playhead reaches it, and at the end of every bar the grid moves forward one generation under Conway's rules. Gliders travel across the grid, blinkers alternate between a run of notes and a chord, and some patterns grow for a while before settling, so the melody keeps changing on its own.

About {{NAME}}

{{NAME}} is a step sequencer driven by Conway's Game of Life. Each lit cell plays its row's note when the playhead reaches its column. At the end of each bar the grid moves forward one generation, so the pattern and the melody change together.

How cells sound

  • Rows are notes, lowest at the bottom. Columns are steps, panned from left to right.
  • New cells are brightest and loudest. Cells that have survived several generations get dimmer, softer and darker in tone.
  • A cell that will go out next generation flickers during playback and plays a short note.
  • Each step plays up to four notes, newest cells first.
  • With Bass on, a low note plays an octave below the lowest lit cell at the start of each bar.
  • Key moves every row up or down together. Swing delays every second step for a looser groove.
  • The Studio engine adds a room reverb and fuller voices. Classic is the original, drier sound.

Saving and sharing

  • Tapes keep up to four patterns on this device, with their sound settings.
  • Copy link puts the pattern and its sound into a link anyone can open.
  • Record audio saves what you hear as a sound file. Export MIDI saves the next eight bars as a MIDI file for your music software.

Drawing

  • Click or drag to light and clear cells. On a touchscreen, tap to toggle one cell, or hold for a moment and then drag to paint.
  • Stamps add a pattern wherever you click, on top of what is already there. Q and E rotate the stamp, Esc puts it away.
  • While playing, any edit holds evolution for one bar so you hear the change first.

The rules

A dark cell with exactly three lit neighbours lights up. A lit cell with two or three lit neighbours stays lit. Every other cell goes dark. This is the rule known as B3/S23. The grid wraps at its edges, so a pattern leaving the right side comes back on the left.
